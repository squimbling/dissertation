BBC News — London & Greater London

Wednesday 28 May 2008

Daughter of Ed Tiley killed in road traffic accident on A316

An urgent appeal has been issued by the London Metropolitan Police following a fatal collision on the A316.

At approximately 10:58pm on 26 May, an unknown vehicle crashed into a silver Peugeot Hybrid 2008, causing a collision into the underpass.

The owner of the vehicle, named locally as Isabel Tiley, was pronounced dead at the scene.

Isabel, who was 23 years old and a Fashion student at Central Saint Martin's College, is believed to have suffered catastrophic brain injuries.

She leaves behind her father, Ed Tiley, a notable London art dealer, and mother, Marisa Tiley-Renaud, daughter of French businessman Jean Renaud.

"She had the sort of energy you could feel from another room," said Tiley-Renaud, when asked for comment.

"Her death will be felt far and wide, but of course it is felt the hardest in our little family.

"I worry I will never be the same person after losing her."

"I will never know a greater loss than losing my daughter, and my grief is only worsened knowing what little information we have on her killer."

"Isabel's death being brought to justice will be the only thing to bring my wife and I any relief in these coming months."

London Metropolitan Police have put out an urgent appeal for witnesses, citing the lack of information on the vehicle that caused the collision as a "serious concern".

"We are potentially dealing with a hit-and-run, and we need to find the driver of the vehicle that caused this collision," said a spokesperson for the London Metropolitan Police.

"We urge anyone with any information to come forward."

Anyone who can assist the police with their investigation is asked to call a designated hotline for Tiley's case on 020 4930 402. All calls will be treated as anonymous. ◀ back

BBC News — London & Greater London

Friday 30 May 2008

Central Saint Martins mourns student killed in car accident

The Central Saint Martins community is mourning the "unfathomable loss" of an MA Fashion student following her death in a fatal road traffic collision.

Isabel Tiley, 23, was pronounced dead at the scene after being killed in a road traffic accident on the A316.

While the investigation is ongoing, the London Metropolitan Police have said progressing the case has proven "exceedingly difficult" due to a lack of leads in identifying the perpetrator.

Tiley attended Central Saint Martins (CSM), where her death has had a profound impact on students and staff alike.

"An unfathomable loss"

Francis Hamilton, Dean of M School at CSM, described Tiley's death as "an unfathomable loss".

"There wasn't a person across the school who didn't know and love Isabel," he said.

"We cannot imagine what her friends, family, and loved ones must be feeling in this unimaginably difficult time, and CSM offers its sincerest condolences.

"We ask that Isabel's family and closest friends have their privacy respected to allow them space to grieve such a loss."

Associate Lecturer Merete Hagen added: "She lit up every room she walked into."

"She was a beautiful girl. She always presented herself immaculately, taking bold risks which reflected her keen eye for fashion.

"But above that, she had such a genuine adoration for fashion. She did everything with such a warm, sunny smile. I think we're all going to miss that."

"The best friend I ever had"

Students on the MA Fashion course are reportedly planning a private in memoriam exhibition celebrating Isabel Tiley's work and life.

The exhibition is expected to feature coursework completed throughout the year, including a posthumous presentation of her Women in the Walls garment collection.

Although dates have yet to be confirmed, it is expected to be held at Lethaby Gallery in Granary Square.

"We want to give Iz the chance to show her hard work off no matter what, and ensure her bright spirit burns on forever," said Jamie Masden, an MA Fashion student and close friend of Tiley.

"She truly was the best friend I ever had, and I know we all felt super close to her on the course.

"She had this way of talking to you which made you feel like you'd known her your entire life."

Another student on the course, Katie Cheung, said Central Saint Martins would not feel the same without Tiley's "infectious energy" and "incredible talent".

"She wanted to start her own label after she graduated and we all wanted to be a part of it," Cheung said.

"If there's one person we all knew would make it, it was Izzy.

"The fact we'll never get to see how huge she becomes is something I still can't come to terms with."

The designated hotline for Tiley's case remains open: 020 4930 402.
