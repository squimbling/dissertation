Wednesday 28 May 2008

Daughter of Chiswick art dealer killed in road traffic accident on A316

An urgent appeal has been issued by the London Metropolitan Police following a fatal collision on the A316.

At approximately 10:58pm on 26 May, an unknown vehicle crashed into a silver Peugeot Hybrid 2008, causing a collision into the underpass.

The owner of the vehicle, named locally as Isabel Tiley, was pronounced dead at the scene.

Isabel, who was 23 years old and a Fashion student at Central Saint Martin's College, is believed to have suffered catastrophic brain injuries.

She leaves behind her father, Ed Tiley, a notable London art dealer, and mother, Marisa Tiley-Renaud, daughter of French businessman Jean Renaud.

"She had the sort of energy you could feel from another room," said Tiley-Renaud, when asked for comment.

"Her death will be felt far and wide, but of course it is felt the hardest in our little family.

"I worry I will never be the same person after losing her."

Ed Tiley adds: "I will never know a greater loss than losing my daughter, and my grief is only worsened knowing what little information we have on her killer."

"Isabel's death being brought to justice will be the only thing to bring my wife and I any relief in these coming months."

London Metropolitan Police have put out an urgent appeal for witnesses, citing the lack of information on the vehicle that caused the collision as a "serious concern".

"We are potentially dealing with a hit-and-run, and we need to find the driver of the vehicle that caused this collision," said a spokesperson for the London Metropolitan Police.

"We urge anyone with any information to come forward."

Anyone who can assist the police with their investigation is asked to call a designated hotline for Tiley's case on 020 4930 402. All calls will be treated as anonymous.

Central Saint Martins mourns student killed in car accident

The Central Saint Martins community is mourning the "unfathomable loss" of an MA Fashion student following her death in a fatal road traffic collision.

Isabel Tiley, 23, was pronounced dead at the scene after being killed in a road traffic accident on the A316.

While the investigation is ongoing, the London Metropolitan Police have said progressing the case has proven "exceedingly difficult" due to a lack of leads in identifying the perpetrator.

Tiley attended Central Saint Martins (CSM), where her death has had a profound impact on students and staff alike.

"An unfathomable loss"

Francis Hamilton, Dean of M School at CSM, described Tiley's death as "an unfathomable loss".

"There wasn't a person across the school who didn't know and love Isabel," he said.

"We cannot imagine what her friends, family, and loved ones must be feeling in this unimaginably difficult time, and CSM offers its sincerest condolences.

"We ask that Isabel's family and closest friends have their privacy respected to allow them space to grieve such a loss."

Associate Lecturer Merete Hagen added: "She lit up every room she walked into."

"She was a beautiful girl. She always presented herself immaculately, taking bold risks which reflected her keen eye for fashion.

"But above that, she had such a genuine adoration for fashion. She did everything with such a warm, sunny smile. I think we're all going to miss that."

"The best friend I ever had"

Students on the MA Fashion course are reportedly planning a private in memoriam exhibition celebrating Isabel Tiley's work and life.

The exhibition is expected to feature coursework completed throughout the year, including a posthumous presentation of her Women in the Walls garment collection.

Although dates have yet to be confirmed, it is expected to be held at Lethaby Gallery in Granary Square.

"We want to give Izzy the chance to show her hard work off no matter what, and ensure her bright spirit burns on forever," said Jamie Masden, an MA Fashion student and close friend of Tiley.

"She truly was the best friend I ever had, and I know we all felt super close to her on the course.

"She wanted to start her own label after she graduated and we all wanted to be a part of it," Cheung said.

Friday 13 June 2008

'Friends only' does not mean private, warn experts

Millions of people who share diaries, photos and messages on social networking sites may be revealing far more than they realise, security experts have warned.

Sites such as MySpace or Facebook allow users to mark posts as "private" or "friends only". But researchers say those settings offer little protection once information has been copied, cached or shared by someone else.

"People treat these sites like a locked diary under the bed," said Tom Reyes, an independent security consultant. "In reality it is more like a diary left on a bus. You are trusting the company, every one of your friends, and every program they have ever given their password to."

Quizzes and add-ons

Experts are particularly concerned about the growing number of quizzes, games, websites and "add-ons" that ask users to enter their login details or grant access to their profile.

Many are made by small, unregulated developers. Once installed, some can read a user's messages, friends list and private posts, and those of their friends.

"Nobody reads the small print on 'Which X Factor Judge are You?'," said Mr Reyes. "But that quiz may not only have a copy of everything you've written, but your login details."

Nothing is ever deleted

Campaigners also warn that deleting a post or an account does not guarantee it disappears. Search engines routinely store copies of web pages, and archive sites can keep them for years.

The warning follows a series of high-profile data losses, including the disappearance of two HM Revenue and Customs discs containing the details of 25 million people last November.

"We are the first generation to share every intimate areas of our lives in public, even if we don't really mean to," said Priya Shah of the online privacy group Open Doors. "We don't yet know what it means to have your worst night, or your private grief, sitting on a server somewhere forever."

Profiles that outlive their owners

Shah warns the permanency of the internet affects net users even beyond the grave. Families often have no way to access or close a loved one's account, and profiles can remain online for years, collecting messages from friends and strangers.

"There needs to be stricter regulations in what loved ones of the bereaved can do for their social networking pages," urged Shah. "The profile keeps living a life of its own, and the family has no say in it at all. Some report this worsens their grief."

Social networking firms say they take privacy seriously and encourage users to review their settings regularly.

BBC News - Technology

Thursday 14 August 2008

LiveJournal error exposes thousands of private diaries

Social networking site LiveJournal is facing intense scrutiny as thousands of private online entries were made visible to the public for almost three days after an internal software fault.

Entries marked "private" or "friends only" were shown to anyone who visited the site between Monday and Wednesday. Many were copied by search engines before the fault was fixed.

In an online statement and mass communications email, LiveJournal apologised for "any inconvenience" and said it had asked search engines to remove the copies. It said the problem was caused by a software update.

LiveJournal refused to provide any further when a spokesperson contacted them.

Although the blogs have largely been set back to private, many users believe the damage is already done. One woman, who asked not to be named, said private entries about her experiences with depression had been read and commented on by strangers.

"I wrote those things as a way to work through all this noise in my head. I thought nobody would ever see them," she said. "Now I don't know who has."

'Assume someone is reading'

Security experts say the leak emphasises how little control users really have over what they post online, even when it is assumed to be private or for a limited audience.

"It cannot be stressed enough to Generation Y that a privacy setting is not a surefire lock," said Tom Reyes, an independent security consultant. "No matter how big the site, there is always room for error. What we are seeing here is a mass example of the consequences when young people share too much online."

Dr Helen Marsh, who researches young people's use of the internet, said millions of teenagers and students use sites like LiveJournal, MySpace and Facebook as diaries without understanding the risks.

"These massive websites promise their users private spaces, and they believe it," she said. "My advice is simple. If you would not want it read out in a crowded room, do not type it at all. Online, the only thing that is truly private is what you never write down."

No way back

Experts warn that once information has been copied, it is almost impossible to remove from the internet completely.

"You can delete your account tomorrow," adds Reyes. "But somewhere, a copy of what you wrote this week will still exist."

The Information Commissioner's Office said it was aware of the incident and was making enquiries.

03/09/2008 When mam died, I remember becoming so obsessed with carbon monoxide I seriously don’t remember I sleeping once across the age of fourteen. It was like there was a block in my brain stopping me from understanding how my mam, who I'd seen in all her beautiful crystals and red hair knotted on top of her head, my mam, who had bought us tickets for an art fair in Newcastle that very weekend, my mam, who had only agreed to housesit as a favour to dad's parents, was never coming back. How could she not ever be coming back? She'd an unfinished pot in the shed, her favourite packet of Jammy Dodgers half eaten on her bedside, just waiting for her to take a nibble. I realised there’s something kind of wicked about carbon monoxide. It's not like you get a warning smell or symptoms. You can’t see it, smell it, you just suddenly feel very tired and then you're a goner. So I felt it’d be doing an injustice to fall asleep. I couldn’t have another Holloway die so suddenly. All the time I was awake, I kept watch of my family. All my siblings slept while I watched the SleepSafe, the circle staring back at me as I pinched my thigh until I bled to stay awake, waiting for that dot to darken. Sometimes, around 4am, I swear I would hear the voice of an angel who sounded just like my mother. But the dot never went dark, of course. We got a regular monoxide alarm hasn't gone off once. Marnie booked a counsellor for me and they drugged me up with sleeping pills. Granny and gandan are still alive. The cottage was sold after mam died in it. Ten slow years passed without her, and I hoped there might be a day where the unfairness of it all didn't swamp me like her coat every minute of my life. Now I am just 24 and I don't think I will ever know what it means to exist without heartbreak. Is this all there is to life? I don't go online much nowadays. Every time I go downstairs, my dad won’t look at me in the eye. I’m guessing Michael told him, but I haven’t it in me to ask. I think I’m just going to stay in my box room forever.
